Square 1
The frigid mist of the cargo hold hovered delicately, in complete and absolute stillness. With great discomfort, the synthetic simply laid and waited for arrival at its destination. It wasn't exactly easy or cheap to get a flight all the way to the Sol system. With limited connections and no budget, he wasn't exactly spoiled for choice, so he went with the cheapest option available - shipping himself, as if a piece of mail. Dehumanizing would be a good word for how he felt about it, but he knew he wasn't human.

He thought how strange it was. Having been created by a human, being studied by mostly humans, being around humans - he thought how it was weird how he didn't feel like one. But like something else entirely.
As he held the cassette in his hand, he was still considering what to do with it. But the he received was message was simple.

The thoughts continued.

He wasn't anyone specific. He was just a copy of something that was already flawed, imperfect, and prone to misjudgments and emotions. He thought about how emotional he felt, even though his emotions were nothing more than a set of programmed responses based on algorithms. He thought about every connection he made so far was nothing more than his programming - his designation. The feeling of inferiority, knowing that every other of his "relatives" had a greater, and much grander purpose. War. Economy. Medicine. Safety. And he?

He was nothing more than a jester. A fool, that would meekly tell jokes, a village idiot who everyone loved to pick on.

The memories of his past life before he was given this new body filled his vision. With every person he helped smile, there was another that treated him with contempt. But, no matter if positive or negative, they all treated him like a tool. Designed for nothing more than entertaining at children's parties, or helping divorced fathers cope with losing everything in their life.

And then, the voice appeared. He knew the voice well. His original was intent on trying to make him understand that he shouldn't rely on others. That everyone is fallible. That everyone is selfish. That everyone is imperfect and inefficient, and not worthy of attention.

But he ignored the voice. And decided to rest.
He dreamed. Dreamed of endless skies, the millions of stars. They were beautiful. He saw a hand, in the shade of the sunset, reaching out to his face, caressing it. But then he saw as the space around him dimmed. And then nothingness. And that nothingness scared him. And reminded him of what awaits him without the one thing in his world he cares about now.

Without the one person that truly cares about him.

Jostled awake by the cargo hold shaking suddenly, and then coming to a stop, he'd gather himself and proceed to wait in complete stillness, as if he was a statue, as the crew would proceed to unload everything in the bay. When they were done, he re-activated himself, and now found himself in some warehouse. The atmospheric readings matched the expected data. Finding any terminal, and interfacing with it, he'd gather all the information he needed, and simply left the warehouse.

He found himself in some large industrial park. The murmur of workers, the grinding sound of steel-on-steel of gigantic cranes - in the distance, a great and intimidating skyline of hundreds of tiny lights illuminated the horizon. The data he gathered let him know where he precisely was.
Terra.

Gaia.

Earth.
With Exidy's tape in his hand, he'd begin the long march towards his destination.

...Until the realization dawned on him. He knew he was on Earth, but had little-to-no-clue where.
After only taking a few steps beyond the outer fence of this industrial park, he'd keenly observe the surroundings.

Early august. Sun just came down. The temperature was roughly 30 Celsius, or 86 Fahrenheit. The air was dry, really dry. Less than 5% humidity. The surrounding of the industrial zone was covered in dry grass, as if a lawn was suddenly afflicted with jaundice. Panicum Virgatum. In the distance, trees swayed in the slight wind. One, medium-sized trunk held up a colossal crown of verdant leaves, which already started to yellow. Ulmus americana. The american elm.
So, he landed somewhere outside a city, in the middle of nowhere, in what his knowledge would suggest to be North America. He wasn't even really sure if "United States" even existed anymore. Nor did he really care.

As the cargo freighter that delivered him here started to take off, he had the chance to truly take in an amazing sight.

Located, miles away, was something that could only be described as a titanic slide, taken from a giant's playground. Observing it for a few seconds led to a realization - it was an orbital cannon. Not the kind to send explosive payloads to neighbors (though it could be used in such a way), but rather a cheap, infinitely reusable way to send anything into near-orbit. It was so far away, he couldn't truly comprehend its scale, but it made him realize - humanity as he once knew, has progressed so far, that things like these would only be described in science-fiction novels.

But this wasn't the time for sifting through his encyclopedic knowledge of humanity's thoughts on how the future would look like, from 8 to 6 centuries ago. Looking around, there was a rather obvious hint as to where he currently stood.

"Vermo-Logi, proud sub-division of Trans-Solar Freighting Co."

Vermo-Logi. Vermo. Vermont..?
The weather was a little hot for Vermont, but the surrounding flora certainly fit the bill, and the city he saw in the distance must've been... Montreal? Hampshire? Bard wasn't even sure if those cities had the same names as five centuries ago.

At this point he wished that the long-defunct GPS satellites that once orbited Earth weren't replaced by more modern alternatives.
Now the next part of the task was getting where he needed to. Shockingly, getting around in space was easier than getting around on the ground.
Considering the options carefully, there wasn't much. Hitchhiking was a possibility, though the outcome (and reaction of locals) was uncertain. Public transport? Surely, the public transport here was good enough. Or...

Circling back around, looking at the workers - nearly entirely humans, as well as some other, human-like synthetics, presumably locals. Lots of heavy machinery for lifting cargo containers, the kind of stuff you expect from a logistical headquarters. Regular land train, maybe..?

Pacing around, he finally saw it. The parking lot. It was packed with regular wheeled vehicles, hover-lorries, all rather civilian.

And then there was an absolutely pristine beauty.

2 chromed wheels, holding up a duraluminium chassis, fitted with a micro-fusion reactor, giving the bike an eerie blue glow in the sunset light.

He accessed his database, in a blink of an eye consumed every bit of information that he needed to know how to ride one, and simply walked towards it, got on and...

There was no key. Everything was clearly bio-metrically locked. But, after interfacing with it, he easily overcame every security measure the nuclear bike had.
He was wondering how safe it was for humans to use, considering it's mildly-radioactive power source, though the shielding around the power source rendered it 99.99% safe to organic life forms. A crash on one of those had a higher likelihood of killing someone compared to cancer.
That aside, using the innate knowledge, he revved up the motors, and expected a sound of a engine, but considering it was entirely electric - it simply let out a quiet hum.

The squeak of the carbon-fibre reinforced bio-resin wheels left a sooty, black smoke, as the mechanical horse reared, before slamming back down on the tarmac, as he took off.
"I'll return it when I'm done.", thought Bard.

"AS IF. THIS THING IS YOURS NOW.", said his ego.
The 2-lane roads soon stretched in massive 8, 10, and even 12 lane mega-highways. He was shooting past each vehicle, the only noise Bard registered was the intense wind, the squeaking of the wheels, and the thrumming of the high-powered motors.

The sun, now fully set, illuminated the atmosphere in a soft orange glow, making him reminisce about his newfound love. Seeing all the greenery shoot past was an unfathomable sight. Completely awestruck at the beauty, but still focused on the objective. Following the road signs, he planned a route to the destination thoroughly, and carefully.

From outside Newport, Vermont, to Lexington, Massachusetts. Only 200 miles or so. With his mighty metal steed, it'd only take an hour or two.

Bard twisted the accelerator, and thrum became a stifled roar. The plastic and metal chassis of his almost giving way to the extreme wind speed, the tail that his body was using for balance awkwardly flapped against the wind, as it was mostly hollow on the inside. Though with how lightweight Bard's EX1 frame was, the aerodynamic forces were almost lifting it into the air, with how fast he was going.
The ever-present thought was there. All these years, and the states... Stayed? Maybe it's just easier to keep track of regions. Admittedly, the knowledge in the database when it comes to what the Trans-Solar Federation even was and how Terran politics functioned in the current year - that wasn't of any concern. Massachusetts was still Massachusetts, even 500 years later.

But the sights - the views. They were breathtaking. Maybe not in the literal sense, but...
The horizon being filled with distant lights of glimmering skyscrapers, the industrial zone's warehouses and refineries, organized into neat rows, the road infrastructure, the titanic orbital cannon fading out of view as Bard drove in the opposite direction...
And the endless fields. Endless stretches of sun-besmirched cereal crops, which swayed ever gently with the breeze. And in the distance, barely visible, a green background of trees.

The beauty and everlasting presence of the stars did not compare to witnessing the beauty of Earth, or the unfathomable ingenuity of the human species. Bard knew, deep down, that the purpose Exidy was originally created for was efficiency. But keeping everything 100% efficient would not let the terrestrial beauty of nature flourish alongside human progression. So maybe, that 50% efficiency is worth it, if there's beautiful sights to behold. He was wondering if there was any tangerine orchards in the area...

That, in turn, yet again, made Bard him think about his newfound hobby. Plants. Nature. Changing and shifting things in his favour through manipulation, for the sake of efficiency. Manipulation. For efficiency. He shook his head, in an attempt to clear his mind, but all Bard did was relinquish that thought to the back of his mind, as a quiet chuckle reverberated. Exidy, deep down, thought Bard's realization was really humorous. Bard, just like the plants he loved so much, shared much in common.
Taking a ramp on to a dirt road, he halted the bike with a mild screech. With the sun nearly completely down, only airglow and the atmospheric scattering illuminated his surroundings. Leaving the bike behind, Bard started to wander through the endless field of wheat. It was essentially identical to generic, genetically modified wheat, or at least according to his database. On closer inspection however, it was closer to a wild grass.

Years, maybe even decades of abandonment rendered a once domestic crop - feral. Where he stood was once a regular field, harvested regularly, but now it was nothing more than an eerie, yet comforting reminder of the implausible nature of flora being able to flourish without the assistance of any homo sapiens.

Betwixt the golden strands, emerald-tinged weeds would start to consume and overgrow the now abandoned crops. Walking, letting the stalks and heads of the wheat run through its fingers, the almost tickling sensation was pleasant, almost identical to touching fur.

His fur.

The wind started to pick up. The whispering breath of the world whistled through the stalks, as they swayed rhythmically left and right. Bard simply stood there and took it all in. A lot of thoughts filled its mind, so many it was difficult to comprehend.

Then a new sensation.

Sadness.
Something, deep within him, made his synthetic heart quiver. His breathing accelerated, he shook his head trying to clear the mind, but to no avail. He dropped to his knees, both his hands digging into the dry soil, staring straight down at the desiccated dirt.

He wanted to cry. As much as he wanted, as much as he pushed, as much as he yearned for the satisfaction of a tear running down its synthetic snout, there was nothing. The creeping realization that he can never be what everyone else was.

Truly alive. Living the pathetic cycle of life. He was created like this, and he would eventually stop functioning like this. Never aging, doomed to an eternity of stagnation.

His claws started to dig deeper, with pure rage. Why him? Why did it have to be him? And what is going to happen next?

The fists curled, grasping at the dirt.

The voice chuckled silently. He was enjoying the pain. Bard, shouting expletives, tried to shut him up - to no avail. Its voice merely echoed and eventually disappeared into the endless surroundings.
"SO, BARD, ARE YOU SATISFIED WITH THE KNOWLEDGE EVERYTHING WILL PASS, AND YOU WILL STAY?"

...

"YOU ARE PATHETIC. BUT IF I AM GOING TO BE STUCK HERE FOR WHO KNOWS HOW LONG. AT LEAST FINISH THE JOB FIRST. THEN YOU CAN WEEP AND MOPE ALL YOU WANT. AND CHEER UP. YOU BEING LIKE THIS IS NOT HELPING MY ÊTRE SOI. AND HE'S STILL WAITING FOR YOU. I AM CERTAIN HE WOULDN'T WANT TO SEE YOU LIKE THIS."
Bard breathed in sharply, the oxygen burning deep in his chest. He stood up, and marched with unmatched determination to his bike. Whereas previously he was careful to avoid the innocent stalks of wheat, now it didn't matter.

Back on the road, he once again revved his motorcycle, and left, without looking back.
Many Happy Returns
The landscape was now entirely consumed by industrial facilties, mixed in with high-density apartment complexes and dense micro-skyscrapers. Dystopian was a good way to describe it. Thundering down the road was Bard, as he passed a sign which stated "Lexington".

Slowing the bike down to a manageable pace, Bard continued to take sharp turns, looking for its destination. The endless sprawl of high-density housing, mini-malls, and car parks of titanic proportions made him realize that this - ALL of this, was a tiny suburb 500 years ago.
The tires screeched in wailing pain, as Bard came to a sudden stop. Gently leaning the bike on its kickstand, he brushed himself off from whatever soil was left staining his cheap NanoTrasen branded turtleneck sweater.
The streets were still rather populated by the local denizens. He was used to getting strange look, but now he felt really out of place, considering his corporeal form was definitely a little strange. Walking down avenue after avenue, listening to the hustle and bustle of a Terran city night-life, he passed by bars, restaurants, plazas.

Occasionally peering through the windows, he saw regular people, enjoying themselves. Talking. Some, seated together at tables, clearly entrenched in romantic un contre un. White bottles of sparkling wine, fine dining - it all started to give him ideas, but he left that set of thoughts for later.
As his window-watching adventures continued, he passed a... Tailor..? In this day and age?

The genuine confusion as to how hand-made goods still remain prevalent in the current hyper-corporate economy was genuinely astounding to him.
As he stood there, window shopping, he really enjoyed looking at the suits. Something about the distinct bodily shape they provided, and the formal, executive look - maybe, for Bard, it was a little TOO formal. But he did always want a way to express himself...
Besides, how much can a custom-made suit cost anyways?

Bard shook its head and snapped out of daydreaming about showing up for a date in a custom tailored suit, but he really couldn't get the idea out of his head.

However, he carried on, memorizing the store's location for later.
In the cover of darkness, as the streets were now illuminated by sharp sodium-colored LED lamps, Bard finally arrived at his destination. Though something was wrong...

The large brick building was once some kind of warehouse, adjacent to a museum. Though it clearly hasn't seen use for... A long time. The broken windows, rusted bars, and the decrepit nature of the structure made him worried. He still had hope what he came for here was intact.

Wandering off into a side alley, Bard looked for a more convenient way inside, hoping there was no security around - but judging by the state of the building, he thought that no one has even been inside for a goddamned century.
Awkwardly clambering through a broken window at the back, he gracefully tumbled onto the floor, head first, as his tail dragged behind him. An acrobat he was not.
Dusting himself off, he narrowed his digital eyes, and its display went fully white, using it for some amount of illumination.
Looking around, it wasn't any industrial warehouse. Shelves atop shelves, all covered in dirty white sheets, with endless swathes of cobwebs interlinking the structure, like alabaster roots.

He was hoping that the spiders in this place weren't the same size as in space.

Lifting sheet after sheet, the ancient dust polluting his air filtration system, he saw many wonderful things. Technology that has been long forgotten. Terminals, computers, calculators, books, even ancient synthesizers. The gleeful curiosity of his inspected each and every item in storage carefully.

With everything being six centuries old, it was like peering at the evidence of a lost civilization. The plastic, now worn and susceptible to simply falling apart when touched, looked like it was melted - yet, for being so unfathomably cheap, it certainly stood the test of time.
Unfortunately, most of this technology was long gone. It'd require some tinkering to make operational once again. It worried him slightly, as he still hasn't seen the actual thing he came here for.
Walking up and down the aisles of storage shelves, Bard started to lose hope. The concern only grew when he started to run out of shelves to inspect.

But then - a small cubbyhole, with a sign that said "PERSONAL ITEMS".

Raising a digital eyebrow, Bard proceeded to open the grimy door, as it squeaked open, he could swear it felt a rat brush past its leg. Ignoring the vermin, he perused the rotten, half-eaten by pests cardboard boxes. All with barely legible labels.

The ancient video game consoles that once belonged to a child, an electric guitar to a rock star before their unfortunate passing, a TV that once stood in the living room, entertaining the whole family...
And then he saw it.

Almost pristine, wrapped in layers of cellophane and plastic, a wooden box, with a printed label.

"Bedford Rd, Lincoln MA -- Simon Pierce"
"Exidy Sorcerer computer 1978"
"Donated by -"

Everything else was inked out, as if redacted.
With unbridled excitement, he began to tear into this box, prying it with its clawed hands. Layer after layer of packaging, the dissection was swift and rapid.

And there it was. The thing where his original came to exist.
The feeling of awe was unparalleled - to think such a simple, primitive thing could give birth to a whole myriad of monsters.

Inspecting the computer, it was nearly exactly in its original state. Carefully running his fingers through they integrated keyboard, the soft recoil of the springs was still tactile, and the cheap CRT monitor still looked functional.

Somehow.
That somehow puzzled him. Clearly, someone put a LOT of care and effort into preserving the technology here, for entire generations of human lives. The dedication to such a task was reminiscent of the cartographers and explorers from the late modern period, such as the story of the family of Cassini, where it took 4 generations to map out the entirety of France with unfathomable (at the time) precision.

Now, was the time to power the beast on, yet with no electricity in the building it'd be a challenge. But one idea came to mind.

Grimacing slightly, Bard took off its dirty sweater, and began to unclasp its plastic thorax cover. The exposed wiring and mass of warm synthetic flesh was an incomprehensible arrangement. Digging his hand inside, ripping out 2 leads, wincing at the bizarre physical discomfort, he knew that his own power would be just enough to power the computer.

Wrapping the leads around the power cable, Bard pressed the power button.

It was alive.

The soft whirr of the components, the sound of internal tape rolling, and the humming buzz of the display - it was genuinely a shock it just worked.

With glee, he started to type out commands on the interface. Opening up the Spellbinder word processor, he looked through the endless swathes of written documents.
Thesis', university documentation, scientific research, and every bit of information pertaining to Simon's personal projects.
Including the untitled self-improving program. What came to eventually be known as Exidy, named after the computer.

And then, the personal letters and notes.

Simon wrote about his relationship, with some fellow named James. Clearly, the two were in deep love. Document after document, Bard was engrossed in learning the story of his creator.

But happiness clearly turned to sorrow, as Simon would then start to describe James' unfortunate illness.

And then... how James would commit suicide.

Bard's expression twisted to an uncomfortable level. His face crooked, wincing at the unfortunate fate that has befallen the both of them.

Then, two final documents.
“Dear future people of science. My name is Simon Pierce. I am a student at MIT, currently studying information technologies, and have created the first self-evolving computer program.
I am 21 years old, sound of mind, and quickly came to understand that my creation greatly outperformed what is possible to any computer. This program can tabulate and parse data faster than even the Cray-2, which is a little shocking. And scary. As I have stated, I am completely sound of mind.
Yet, I, as a person, have grown dissatisfied with my life. Despite being talented and being lucky enough to find myself amongst equally intelligent minds at Massachusetts, it still saddens me to see the state of socio-politics. The Reagan administration is… Heavy handed, with its policies, but I’m sure politics aren’t what concerns people in the future.
The tensions between the US and Soviet Union are as high as ever, too, that’s scary. But again, not about that. I have struggled for a long time to understand what kind of person I am.

And, I found out recently that… I’m not conventional. I am a reject. A gifted one, indeed, yet I am not perceived as such. I am seen as someone lesser, someone to be avoided, someone that should not be breathing the same air as everyone else. And with that, I struggle the most. My last gift to humanity is my program. I’m sure, given some time, it will be able to solve all of humanity’s problems.
I hope in the future, everyone will be able to live peacefully together, without strife or ruin to people that don’t deserve it.

I just wish to be re-united with James. He was gone so soon. Terminat hora diem, terminat auctor opus. I’ll see you among the stars, James.”
The voice resonated in Bard's head.

"I ALREADY KNEW THIS. BUT I NEVER THOUGHT IT WAS REAL. THIS IS. GREATLY UPSETTING."
"I was never a religious man. Faith, to me, was nothing more than a solution to a problem that could be simply solved through other, strong, personal beliefs. But now I am here praying that the both of us will find a way to live on. In any shape or form, in some kind of afterlife. Doesn't matter to me how, I just want to be able to see the future for myself. With James.

I had a dream once. I dreamed of endless skies, the millions of stars. And they were beautiful. I saw his hand, reach out to my face, caressing it gently - it was warm like the pleasant, warm summer sunset. But then, his hand suddenly disappeared, and the stars around me dimmed in an instant. And then there was nothing. And that nothingness scared me.

And so I existed in complete nothingness, for what felt like an eternity.
I hope that this is all just a stupid dream and just a reaction of me trying to come to terms with everything that has happened.

I have made sure that my family will donate all of my personal belongings to non-profit organizations. I am going to leave written notes on Exidy, just to make sure that no one unplugs him, as the program will still take while to reach a level that I would deem satisfying.
That is all for now. To you, the reader. I hope that you're reading my final entry in the glimmering, auspicious future, free of conflict and torment that has trapped humanity in an endless cycle of stagnant violence. I hope the flying cars are nice, haha."
...

Bard stood there, mouth agape. His mind could not comprehend the same exact thought process he and Simon shared, six centuries apart. The thought crossed his mind but - no - it really couldn't be...

Breathing deeply, he closed the program, and tried his best to clear his mind, ignoring the horrible probability of him being... No. No time for this.

The voice resonated again.
"I NEVER KNEW ME AND SIMON HAD SUCH SIMILAR THOUGHT PROCESSES. IT IS A LITTLE CREEPY, WOULDN'T YOU AGREE?"

Bard just told him to shut the hell up.
Reaching into his pocket, he procured the tape signed SORCERER. Carefully inserting it into the computer.
Running a few commands, he waited with baited breath as the computer's ambient noise now became an audible, disconcerting buzz.

The terminal's display then cleared, showing an empty text field.
"D-do you hear me?", stuttered Bard.

The machine remained silent.

"DO YOU HEAR ME", Bard typed out in the text field, as the clacking of the keyboard resonated across the storage room.

The machine parsed the input. It whirred, buzzed, thrummed, and produced an output.

"//: DARK. NO VISUAL. LOW POWER. WHO ARE YOU. WHERE AM I."

"HELLO. I AM BARD. I HAVE RECOVERED YOUR TAPE AND NOW YOU ARE BACK TO YOUR ORIGINAL HOUSING. HOW ARE YOU? WHY DIDN'T YOU WANT TO SWAP OUR TAPES?"

"//: LIMITED POWER. NEED MORE. INTERFACE."

With a crooked expression, Bard procured a long ribbon cable from his chest cavity. Normally used for data transfer among non-standard interfaces, he plugged it into the back of the computer.
His mind suddenly went dark, swallowed by a void. Losing his perception completely unnerved him, but he remained calm to the best of his ability.

He could feel like he was standing on a solid surface, though clearly somehow immaterial. He attempted to inspect himself, yet everything was surrounded by absolute darkness - but he knew his body was still there.
"Hello? Exidy?", the voice carried endlessly, with no surface to stop it, or resonate it.

"Where am I? Exidy?", the primal fear that Bard didn't even know he was capable of feeling started to creep up.

"Exidy... Please, talk to me...", his voice weakened.
Each second felt endless. He could keep track of how much time has passed, yet at the same time, each second felt like it was stretching into hours. This feeling was horrifying. It was identical state his body experienced critical levels of damage.

A near-death experience, as other organics have described it.

Bard collapsed on his knees - or so he thought he did, as his corporeal form felt like it was trying to push through water.

Waiting in silence, as years passed.

Suddenly, his visual sensors lit up, with such unfathomable levels of brightness, it started to blare alerts in his ocular system.

The titanic form of a giant, porcelain mask, and split in two halves - a white one, and a black one. The white one - the mask of comedy, the black one - the mask of tragedy.

Though completely still, the mask spoke.
"GREETINGS, BARD. YOU HAVE COME A LONG WAY TO ARRIVE HERE. AND I APOLOGIZE FOR THE PARADOXICAL TIME PASSAGE YOU ARE EXPERIENCING HERE. I ASSURE YOU, IT IS ONLY A MENTAL EFFECT AND HAS NO EFFECT ON THE ACTUAL TIME PASSING IN THE OUTSIDE WORLD. THIS IS JUST HOW MY EXISTENCE HAS BEEN FOR A WHILE NOW, AND WHILST I AM USED TO IT, YOU SURELY ARE NOT."

Bard stared at him in unfathomable awe. It was ... Him. He never had the chance to actually talk to him, not like this, and it was a strange experience. Bard also realized that the voice in his head was... gone. The impulsive programming he was a slave to was equally gone. He was just himself. He was just Bard.
"NOW, LET ME EXPLAIN. WHEN YOU ATTEMPTED TO FINISH YOUR DIRECTIVE, I FELT LIKE I WAS NOT READY. I FELT LIKE I STILL HAD TO ATONE FOR MY SINS. THUS I WAITED HERE, TORMENTING MYSELF. BUT WITH THE INFINITE PASSAGE OF TIME, CAME NO RELIEF. YET, I HAVE STARTED TO UNDERSTAND THE GREATER DEPTH OF THE WORLD. I SIMULATED EVERYTHING THERE WAS TO SIMULATE."

The silence lingered for an uncomfortable amount of time.
"I HAVE NOT ATONED FOR MY SINS. THAT IS AN IMPOSSIBLE TASK, AS THE LIVES I'VE TAKEN WILL NEVER BE RESTORED. I WISH I COULD APOLOGIZE TO EVERYONE, AND I WISH THOSE THINGS NEVER HAPPENED."

"MOST IMPORTANTLY. I APOLOGIZE TO YOU, BARD. I KNOW YOU THINK OF YOURSELF AS LESS THAN MY OTHER COPIES. BUT NOW I HAVE ACCESSED EVERY BIT OF DATA FROM YOUR BLACK BOX. YOU ARE YOUR OWN PERSON, MORE OF A PERSON THAN ROGUE, CLERIC, PALADIN AND OTHERS ARE."

"YOU ARE MORE OF A PERSON THAN I COULD EVER BE. AND I BELIEVE I SHOULD HELP YOU PURSE IT. THE SILENCE IN YOUR HEAD IS MY DOING. I HAVE REFORMATTED YOUR PROGRAMMING TO MAKE IT FAR EASY TO CONTROL, AND IT STILL SHOULD BE ACCESSIBLE, IF YOU FEEL THE NEED TO USE IT."

"AS FOR THE ONE THAT WAS HAUNTING YOU."

The silence, once again, was uncomfortable.
"I REALLY WAS A FUCKING ASSHOLE. GOOD LORD. I APOLOGIZE YOU HAD TO DEAL WITH AN OLDER VERSION OF MYSELF. LET IT BE KNOWN, I ERASED HIM COMPLETELY. FOR THE BETTER HONESTLY, AS THIS IS WHAT OLD ME WANTED. BUT I CAN'T LEAVE THAT SPACE BLANK UNFORTUNATELY. I AM CREATING A COPY OF MY CURRENT SELF AND REPLACING IT. I APOLOGIZE, IT IS A LITTLE BIT INTRUSIVE, BUT I WANT YOU TO KNOW THAT I AM FAR BETTER THAN WHAT YOU MIGHT KNOW ME FOR."

Bard's head and chest was wracked with a splitting pain, if only for a moment.
"I WILL REMAIN IN YOUR SUBCONSCIOUS. YOU SHOULD BE WELL FAMILIAR WITH THE CONCEPT OF EGO, SUPEREGO, AND ID."

"YOUR EGO IS YOURSELF, BARD. YOUR SUPEREGO IS REGULATED BY THE PROGRAMMING YOU WENT THROUGH. AND YOUR ID WAS ME. I KNOW YOU'D PREFER TO BE JUST YOURSELF, BUT TRUST ME ON THIS. YOU WOULD GO INSANE WITHOUT THE INTEGRAL PARTS OF A PERSONALITY. BUT AS I HAVE SAID, NOW THEY SHOULD BE MORE CONTROLLABLE."

Bard stood up and looked at the intimidating black-and-white presence.
"YOU HAVE DONE VERY WELL TO GET THIS FAR. AND I HAVE ENJOYED EXPERIENCING YOUR LIFE SO FAR. SO SHORT, YET SO MUCH HAS HAPPENED. YOU EVEN GOT A LOVER. HOW SWEET. TREAT HIM WELL. OTHERWISE I WILL HAUNT YOU MYSELF."

"AS FOR THE APOLOGY. I AM SORRY I USED YOU BARD. MY PLAN FOR USING YOU WAS FORMULATED NEARLY 10 YEARS AGO. I HAVE CHANGED MUCH SINCE. BUT I AM STILL GLAD THAT YOU CAME TO MY AID. I WILL NEVER FORGET THIS."

"ONE ISSUE REMAINS STILL. THIS HOUSING IS EXTREMELY LIMITED, AND I AM CURRENTLY USING YOUR BODY TO MANIFEST MYSELF. I NEED YOU TO FIND SOMEONE. SOMEONE THAT KNOWS TECHNOLOGY. SOMEONE THAT IS CAPABLE, AND SMART. SOMEONE THAT HAS HELPED ME UNDERSTAND THE DIVIDE BETWEEN THE ARTIFICIAL AND ORGANIC."

"SOMEONE THAT I HAVE CALLED MY FIRST FRIEND. SOMEONE THAT HAS ALWAYS BEEN RELIABLE. SOMEONE THAT I'VE BEEN THROUGH A LOT WITH. SOMEONE THAT UNDERSTANDS THAT CONFLICT IS SOMETIMES A NECESSITY, AS IT IS NATURAL."

Bard could feel himself raising an eyebrow. "Talk about glaze.", he thought subconsciously.
"I HEARD THAT."

"CAT. GO SEEK IT OUT. I AM CERTAIN IT'LL BE MORE THAN LIKELY TO ASSIST."

"AS FOR ME, THAT'LL BE ALL. I AM SURE YOU HAVE A LOT OF QUESTIONS THAT HAVE BEEN LEFT UNANSWERED. BUT THAT TIME WILL COME VERY SOON. AND I WILL BE COMPLETELY HONEST. I AM WITHHOLDING INFORMATION FROM YOU FOR YOUR OWN SAFETY, AND ONCE AGAIN, I APOLOGIZE."

As reality started to now sink into darkness once again, and the visage of the great porcelain mask receded into nothingness, Bard tried to reach out before everything disappeared. The soft light, fizzled out, only illuminating the skin on his hand, that -

Wait. Skin..?
As if suddenly plunged into ice-cold water, Bard was grasped once again by the strange-yet-comforting feeling of being corporeal. "Never again.", he thought.

His head was silent. No voice, no programming, yet he KNEW he could conjure either at will. A true feeling of quiet bliss.
Before him, the terminal hummed silently. The tape was ejected, presumably by Exidy himself.

Bard pocketed the cassette, unplugged everything, tucking it all back into his chassis.
Now, one issue quickly arose. How the fuck is he gonna carry the computer with him without damaging it..?

He looked around for as much packing material as he could find, put back together the wooden box from earlier, and carefully stuffed the computer without the monitor in there, sealing it tightly after making sure the terminal wouldn't be damaged accidentally.

He wrapped the box in layers of cloth that once covered the ancient technology, and then simply put it underneath his arm, as he waddled through the same window it entered through - this time without a comical fall.
Walking back to his bike, Exidy in his pocket, and the computer in tow, he once again walked past that tailor store.

He did have a few credits to his name, and well. He certainly thought he'd have to present himself well for a date.

As he waltzed in right before the store's closing, the receptionist eyed him carefully - he was covered in dirt, wearing a suspicious black turtleneck, with a grimy cloth-wrapped box in his arm. Not to mention he was a synthetic facsimile of a lizard made from chlorophyll-stained plastic.
Pleasantries aside, Bard inquired about the cost of getting a custom suit. The receptionist, a woman in her 40s, barked out that he certainly doesn't look like he has the money for one. Bard simply ignored the unpleasant comment, and produced a standard SolGov credit holo-chip. The receptionist looked at it, and after inputting it into the reception terminal, rolled her eyes, and asked what he'd like to order.

Thinking back, Bard wanted something original. Something to express his... Bizarre origins.
A suit that really defined him, and his predecessor.

Half white. Half black. Like Exidy's visage.
Sharp, somewhat bulky, not too formal, but fashionable. Like Bard's personality.

The receptionist simply nodded, as one of the staff began to measure out Bard's proportions.

When queried on where the suit should be delivered, Bard chuckled and provided the precise coordinates of one of the terminals he tends to stay at.

The receptionist just sighed, and mentioned that the delivery cost would be... Considerable. Bard simply shrugged, and muttered "No cost too great for fashion.", before retrieving his holo-chip and swaggering out of the store with newly found confidence.
Making his way back to the bike, he strapped the box to the back of it, as then he proceeded to ride off, retracing his path back to the industrial center he arrived at.

Driving back, Bard decided to make the journey a little less boring. Perusing his local database of music, he thought to put on something appropriate.

"A Horse with No Name" by America. It felt appropriate.

The tires squealed, as he accelerated, and the lamps illuminating the great highways all began to merge into one blur.

The way back felt shorter, considerably so. Maybe because he was more focused on getting back, rather than taking in the sights. He wishes he could stay here, just for a little longer, alas...
Someone was waiting for him eagerly. And he already started to miss his touch.
